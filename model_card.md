Reflection 

Limitations/Biases

The program is extremley transparent. Produces explainations and scores based on very basic data. As a result it has no learning behavior and will produce the same results for the same task inputs regardles of a user's habits. Ther is also a bias when it comes to priority. The system will increase the score for low-priority tasks but not for high-priority task. There is also a bias in history. The more a task has been marked late, the more pesimistic the program becomes. 

Misuse

Users may become to reliant on the scheduler. So they might constantly ignore task just because the AI deemed it low priority. Users may also use the program for medical decisions like skipping medication because it was also not flagged as high risk. For misuse like these, it will be essential to include disclaimers and clearly defined purposes so that users know how to properly utilize the app. 

Surprises

What surprised me the most when testing the AI's relaibilty is how many times the AI can be wrong or make mistakes. Most people see AI as the new google with accuate information all the time, but there have been many time when I had to correct the AI on information or errors. 

Collaboration

I utilized the Ai as a guide and another set of eyes when I got stuck. Especially for the system design where I had not clue where to start or how to organize the data at all, I would use the AI for better direction. The AI gave the helpful suggestion of implementing a numeric risk score rather than just the category. However, it was flwaed when going over the recurrence feature. With the recurrence feature, it will assign the same risk score daily even if the task was done on time.
